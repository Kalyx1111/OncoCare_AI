Cancer Conditions

Comprehensive clinical research from NCCN, ESMO, ASCO, WHO and ICMR guidelines.

⚠
All information is for educational research. Cancer diagnosis and treatment require multidisciplinary tumour board discussion, histopathology, and molecular profiling. Never delay urgent oncology assessment.

Quick Clinical Reference

Neutropenic Fever Recognition

  • Temp ≥38.3°C once or ≥38.0°C sustained + ANC <0.5
  • MUST treat with IV antibiotics within 1 hour of presentation
  • Piperacillin-tazobactam first-line; add vancomycin if catheter/skin infection
  • NEVER wait for blood culture results before starting antibiotics

Spinal Cord Compression Red Flags

  • Back pain worse lying down, at night, with coughing/straining
  • New leg weakness, sensory loss, bladder/bowel dysfunction (late sign)
  • Dexamethasone 16mg IV IMMEDIATELY if suspected — before imaging
  • MRI whole spine within 24 hours (same-day if rapid deterioration)

Biomarker-Driven Treatment

  • Breast: ER/PR/HER2, BRCA1/2, Ki-67
  • Lung: EGFR, ALK, ROS1, KRAS G12C, PD-L1 TPS
  • Colorectal: RAS/BRAF, MSI-H/dMMR, HER2
  • Universal: TMB-high and MSI-H predict immunotherapy response across tumour types

India Cancer Priorities

  • Oral cancer: leading cause is smokeless tobacco (gutka, khaini, pan masala)
  • Cervical cancer: 2nd most common in Indian women — HPV vaccination priority
  • Late-stage presentation (60-70% Stage III/IV) drives survival gap vs West
  • Generic targeted therapy (imatinib, trastuzumab biosimilars) improves access
Most Common Cancer

Breast Cancer

Research on molecular subtypes, staging, surgery, and systemic therapy from NCCN, ESMO and ASCO guidelines.

Molecular Subtypes and Diagnosis

Most common cancer in women globally, approximately 2.3 million new cases annually. India: approximately 178,000 cases/year, median age 52 (younger than West). 5-year survival: 90% (Stage I) down to 28% (Stage IV).

Molecular Subtypes

  • Luminal A (ER+/PR+, HER2-, Ki-67 low): Best prognosis, often endocrine therapy alone
  • Luminal B (ER+/PR+, HER2- high Ki-67, or HER2+): Intermediate, endocrine + chemo
  • HER2-enriched (ER-/PR-/HER2+): Anti-HER2 targeted therapy essential
  • Triple-Negative (TNBC, ER-/PR-/HER2-): Most aggressive; chemo + immunotherapy; BRCA1/2 testing mandatory

Diagnosis: Triple Assessment

Clinical examination + Imaging (mammogram gold-standard, ultrasound for dense/young breast, MRI for high-risk) + Biopsy (core needle preferred). BI-RADS 1-6 classification. SLNB standard for axillary staging in early breast cancer.

Surgery and Radiotherapy

  • Breast-conserving surgery (BCS/lumpectomy) + radiotherapy: Equivalent survival to mastectomy for suitable tumours
  • Mastectomy: For larger tumours, multicentric disease, or prophylactic (BRCA1/2 carriers)
  • SLNB: Standard axillary staging; avoids full clearance morbidity if node-negative
  • Axillary lymph node dissection (ALND): If sentinel node positive
  • Radiotherapy: After BCS (reduces local recurrence 50-70%); post-mastectomy for T3/T4 or node-positive

Systemic Therapy

  • Chemotherapy: Anthracyclines (doxorubicin/epirubicin) + taxanes (paclitaxel/docetaxel) — adjuvant/neoadjuvant backbone
  • Anti-HER2: Trastuzumab 1 year adjuvant; pertuzumab+trastuzumab (neoadjuvant/metastatic); T-DM1 for residual disease; trastuzumab deruxtecan (T-DXd) for metastatic HER2+/HER2-low
  • Endocrine therapy: Tamoxifen 5-10 years (premenopausal); aromatase inhibitors (postmenopausal); CDK4/6 inhibitors (palbociclib, ribociclib, abemaciclib) + AI for metastatic HR+/HER2-
  • PARP inhibitors: Olaparib, talazoparib for germline BRCA-mutated HER2- advanced disease
  • TNBC: Pembrolizumab for high-risk early-stage; chemotherapy backbone

Screening

  • Mammography: annually 40-50, every 2 years 50-74 (varies by guideline)
  • India: opportunistic mammography, no organised national programme
  • High-risk (BRCA1/2): annual MRI + mammogram from age 25-30
  • TP53 carriers: annual MRI from age 20
  • Breast awareness/self-examination important especially in low-resource settings
Leading Cancer Death

Lung Cancer

Research on NSCLC/SCLC, molecular targets, and immunotherapy from NCCN, ESMO and ASCO guidelines.

Lung Cancer Overview

Leading cause of cancer death globally (~1.8 million deaths/year). NSCLC ~85% (adenocarcinoma 40%, squamous 30%, large cell 15%), SCLC ~15%. India: ~72,000 cases/year; 30-40% in never-smokers (vs <10% West) — particularly younger non-smoking women with EGFR mutations.

Treatment by Stage

  • Early (I-II): Surgical resection (lobectomy), adjuvant chemo for Stage II-IIIA; adjuvant osimertinib for EGFR+ resected disease
  • Stage III: Concurrent chemoradiation, then consolidation durvalumab 12 months (PACIFIC trial)
  • Stage IV with driver mutation: Targeted therapy (osimertinib, alectinib, etc.)
  • Stage IV no driver mutation: Pembrolizumab ± chemotherapy based on PD-L1 status
  • SCLC: Immunotherapy (atezolizumab/durvalumab) + carboplatin+etoposide (extensive); chemoradiation + PCI (limited)

Screening

LDCT annually for high-risk: age 50-80, ≥20 pack-years, currently smoking or quit within 15 years. Reduces mortality 20-24% (NLST, NELSON trials). Not yet systematically implemented in India.

Rising in India

Colorectal Cancer

Research on hereditary syndromes, staging, and treatment from NCCN, ESMO and ASCO guidelines.

Overview and Hereditary Syndromes

3rd most common cancer globally, 2nd leading cause of cancer death. Rising in urban India due to Westernised diets. 75% sporadic, 25% familial. Lynch syndrome (MMR mutations, 70-80% lifetime risk), FAP (APC mutation, near 100% risk without colectomy).

Diagnosis and Staging

Colonoscopy gold-standard. MRI pelvis essential for rectal cancer surgical planning (CRM assessment). CEA for monitoring, not diagnosis.

Treatment

  • Colon: surgical resection + adjuvant FOLFOX/CAPOX for Stage III
  • Rectal: total mesorectal excision (TME) + neoadjuvant chemoradiation for T3/T4 or node-positive
  • Watch-and-wait for complete response after neoadjuvant therapy (dMMR/MSI-H — remarkable response to pembrolizumab)
  • Metastatic: FOLFOX/FOLFIRI ± bevacizumab/cetuximab (RAS/BRAF WT); pembrolizumab 1st-line for MSI-H/dMMR

Screening

Colonoscopy every 10 years from age 50 (gold standard), or FIT annually, or CT colonography every 5 years. Lynch syndrome: annual colonoscopy from 20-25 years. India: no organised national programme.

Haematological Malignancies

Research on leukaemias, lymphomas, myeloma, and stem cell transplant from NCCN, ESMO and ASCO guidelines.

Leukaemias

AML: Medical emergency. Intensive induction (7+3), consolidation with allo-SCT for high-risk. FLT3 inhibitors (midostaurin), IDH inhibitors (ivosidenib, enasidenib), venetoclax+azacitidine for older/unfit. APL (PML-RARA): ATRA+ATO curative in 90%+.

ALL: Common in children. Philadelphia-positive: TKI-based regimen; blinatumomab and inotuzumab for relapsed/refractory.

CML: BCR-ABL TKIs (imatinib 1st-gen; dasatinib, nilotinib 2nd-gen). Treatment-free remission achievable in up to 50% of deep responders.

CLL: Watch-and-wait for asymptomatic; BTK inhibitors (ibrutinib, acalabrutinib, zanubrutinib) 1st-line and relapsed; venetoclax+obinutuzumab fixed-duration.

Lymphomas

Hodgkin Lymphoma: ABVD standard; BV-AVD for advanced (ECHELON-1); PD-1 inhibitors for relapsed. Cure rates 85-90%.

DLBCL: R-CHOP standard; Pola-R-CHP superior PFS (POLARIX); CAR-T for relapsed/refractory — potentially curative.

Follicular Lymphoma: Indolent; R-CHOP or BR; watch-and-wait for low-burden asymptomatic disease.

Multiple Myeloma: VRd induction + ASCT for eligible; Dara-VRd superior PFS/OS (PERSEUS). BCMA-targeted: belantamab mafodotin, CAR-T (ide-cel, cilta-cel), teclistamab.

Stem Cell Transplant and CAR-T

Allogeneic SCT: Potentially curative for AML, ALL, MDS, CML. Requires HLA-matched donor. GvHD and infection are key complications.

Autologous SCT: DLBCL (relapsed), myeloma, Hodgkin lymphoma.

CAR-T cell therapy: CD19-CAR-T (axi-cel, tisa-cel) for B-cell ALL/DLBCL; BCMA-CAR-T (ide-cel, cilta-cel) for myeloma. CRS and ICANS are key toxicities managed in specialist centres.

Cutting Edge

Immunotherapy & Targeted Therapy

Research on checkpoint inhibitors, targeted therapy, ADCs, and CAR-T/bispecifics from NCCN, ESMO and ASCO guidelines.

Immune Checkpoint Inhibitors

Biggest oncology revolution since chemotherapy. PD-1 inhibitors (pembrolizumab, nivolumab), PD-L1 inhibitors (atezolizumab, durvalumab), CTLA-4 (ipilimumab). Biomarkers: PD-L1 TPS/CPS, TMB-high, MSI-H/dMMR (tumour-agnostic approval).

Immune-Related Adverse Events (irAEs)

Pneumonitis, colitis, hepatitis, endocrinopathies (thyroid most common), dermatitis, myocarditis (rare but life-threatening). Grade 3-4: high-dose steroids, hold ICI, specialist involvement.

Targeted Therapy Classes

  • EGFR/ALK/BCR-ABL TKIs, CDK4/6 inhibitors, BTK inhibitors, PARP inhibitors
  • KRAS G12C inhibitors (sotorasib, adagrasib) — first direct KRAS inhibitors after 40 years
  • VEGF/VEGFR inhibitors (bevacizumab, sunitinib, cabozantinib)
  • HER2-targeted (trastuzumab, T-DM1, T-DXd)

Antibody-Drug Conjugates (ADCs)

Targeted delivery of cytotoxic payload via monoclonal antibody. T-DXd (HER2+ and HER2-low breast — DESTINY-Breast04 revolutionised care for HER2-low patients). Sacituzumab govitecan (TNBC, urothelial). Key toxicity: interstitial lung disease with T-DXd — monitor closely.

CAR-T Cells and Bispecific Antibodies

CAR-T: CD19-directed (axi-cel, tisa-cel) for lymphoma/ALL; BCMA-directed (ide-cel, cilta-cel) for myeloma. Toxicities: CRS (tocilizumab), ICANS (steroids).

Bispecifics: blinatumomab (CD19xCD3, ALL), teclistamab (BCMAxCD3, myeloma), glofitamab (CD20xCD3, DLBCL). Step-up dosing manages CRS risk.

Cytotoxic Therapy

Chemotherapy & Side Effect Management

Research on common regimens, toxicity grading, and management from NCCN and ASCO guidelines.

Toxicity Management

  • Nausea/vomiting: Triple antiemetics (NK1+5HT3+dex) for highly emetogenic regimens (cisplatin, AC)
  • Neutropenia: Febrile neutropenia is an ONCOLOGICAL EMERGENCY — treat within 1 hour
  • Peripheral neuropathy: Duloxetine 60mg is the only evidence-based treatment for CIPN
  • Cardiotoxicity: Anthracycline cumulative dose limits; monitor LVEF; dexrazoxane cardioprotective
  • Fatigue: Most prevalent side effect; exercise is the most evidence-based intervention

Oncology Emergencies

🚨
ONCOLOGY EMERGENCIES — 112 (India) / 999 (UK) / 911 (US): Neutropenic fever (treat within 1 hour), spinal cord compression (dexamethasone immediately), SVC syndrome, tumour lysis syndrome, hypercalcaemia of malignancy. ALL LIFE-THREATENING.

🚨 Febrile Neutropenia

Temp ≥38.3°C or ≥38.0°C sustained + ANC <0.5. MUST TREAT WITHIN 1 HOUR.

  • MASCC score determines risk (≥21 low-risk)
  • High-risk: IV piperacillin-tazobactam immediately; add vancomycin if catheter/skin infection
  • Low-risk: oral ciprofloxacin+amoxicillin-clavulanate, close monitoring
  • G-CSF for high-risk features

🚨 Spinal Cord Compression

Back pain worse lying/coughing + new weakness/sensory loss/bladder dysfunction.

  • Dexamethasone 16mg IV IMMEDIATELY — before imaging
  • Urgent MRI whole spine within 24 hours
  • Multidisciplinary: spinal surgery vs radiotherapy

🚨 SVC Syndrome

Facial/arm swelling, plethora, headache worse bending, stridor (severe).

  • Dexamethasone 16mg IV to reduce oedema
  • SVC stenting for rapid symptom relief
  • Chemoradiotherapy for chemosensitive tumours (SCLC, lymphoma)

🚨 Tumour Lysis Syndrome

Hyperkalaemia + hyperphosphataemia + hyperuricaemia + hypocalcaemia after treatment of high-burden disease.

  • Prevention: IV hydration + allopurinol or rasburicase before treatment
  • High-risk: Burkitt lymphoma, ALL, bulky DLBCL, venetoclax in CLL
  • 4-6 hourly electrolyte monitoring for high-risk patients
Supportive Oncology

Palliative Care

Research on pain management, psychosocial support, and end-of-life care from WHO and NICE guidelines.

WHO Analgesic Ladder

  • Step 1 (mild): Paracetamol, NSAIDs
  • Step 2 (moderate): Codeine, tramadol, low-dose morphine
  • Step 3 (severe): Oral morphine (first-line), oxycodone, transdermal fentanyl (once stable)
  • Adjuvants: Amitriptyline/gabapentin (neuropathic), bisphosphonates/single-fraction RT (bone pain)
  • Laxative MANDATORY with all opioids

Early Palliative Care Integration

Landmark Temel 2010 NEJM trial: early palliative care alongside oncological treatment in metastatic NSCLC improved quality of life AND survival. Not just end-of-life — integrated from diagnosis.

India Context

Oncology in India

India-specific cancer burden, treatment access, and prevention from ICMR data and national guidelines.

Cancer Burden (ICMR NCDIR 2022)

~1.46 million new cases in 2022. Top cancers: Breast (14.2%), Cervix (9.4%), Oral cavity (9.3%), Lung (7.5%), Colorectal (5.5%). India-specific: very high oral cancer (gutka/tobacco chewing), gallbladder cancer (3rd most common in North Indian women), 39% of all cancers tobacco-attributable.

Treatment Access

Tata Memorial Centre Mumbai (Asia's largest), AIIMS network, PMJAY insurance (up to Rs 5 lakh). Generic drugs significantly reduce cost (imatinib generic 200-500 INR/month vs 3000-4000 brand). Challenges: Tier-1 city concentration, 6-9 month diagnostic delays, 70% out-of-pocket health spend.

Prevention

Tobacco control (267 million users), HPV vaccination (Cervavac India-made), National Cancer Screening Programme (oral/cervical/breast in PHCs). Tobacco Cessation Helpline: 1800-11-2356.
